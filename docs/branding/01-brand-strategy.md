# Brand strategy

Version 1 · October 19, 2026 · Working product name: Transmogrifier.

## The idea

**An app for the moment.**

Some needs last an evening. Some last a weekend. They still deserve a useful little app. Describe what you need, try what gets made, and change it in ordinary words. Keep it while it helps. Delete it when you are done.

The product makes small, personal tools feel worth making, even when they will only be used once.

## Positioning

For people who have a small task and no interest in learning software development, Transmogrifier turns everyday descriptions into personal apps they can use on their Android device. It makes room for short-lived needs: tonight's game, this weekend's trip, or the decision taking longer than it should.

Category descriptor: **A little app maker for everyday life.**

Primary tagline: **Little apps. For right now.**

Campaign line: **Make it. Use it. Move on.**

“Quick” and “easy” are design objectives. Avoid a promised number of seconds, a guaranteed first result, or an implication of zero setup.

## Audience

The audience knows how to use a phone, but should not need to understand code, prompts, models, or AI services. Write for adults with ordinary needs and limited patience. Age and occupation are less useful than the moment that creates a need.

| Moment | What someone wants | Example app | What success feels like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Game night | Track points without arguing | Two-team scorekeeper on one phone | “That did exactly what we needed.” |
| Weekend away | Remember what to pack | A checklist they can change and check off | “I made it fit this trip.” |
| Lunch indecision | Pick from a few options | Random picker using names they enter | “Finally. Lunch.” |
| Hosting friends | Keep track of jobs | A prep checklist on the host's device | “One less thing in my head.” |
| A passing curiosity | Try a tiny idea | A counter or simple game | “That was worth a try.” |

These are example requests, not bundled templates or verified output guarantees. The group examples use one device; they do not imply shared accounts or live collaboration.

## Brand promise and proof

| Promise | Experience that supports it | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Say what you need | Create with a plain-language description | Current setup still requires an AI provider key |
| Make it fit | Ask for follow-up changes | Generated results can need another attempt |
| Use it here | Open the result inside the Android app | No standalone store publication or public sharing is promised |
| Move on when you like | Rename, keep, or manually delete an app | Apps do not expire or disappear automatically |

Emotional benefit: permission to make something small without committing to a new hobby, a software project, or a permanent place on the home screen.

## Personality

Bright, resourceful, lightly cheeky, and clear. The brand is a capable friend who has a useful idea and gets out of the way. Humor lives in the situation: score disputes, overpacked bags, and deciding on lunch. It never targets the user's skill or intelligence.

The visual world is colorful and tactile. Everyday utility gives it an adult center. No mascot is required.

## What “throw-away” means

Use “for right now,” “for tonight,” and “keep it while it helps” in public copy. “Throw-away apps” remains useful internal shorthand, but can suggest poor quality, waste, or automatic data erasure when used as a headline.

Short-lived usefulness is a valid outcome. Keeping an app is equally valid. Do not pressure people to delete, build daily, maintain streaks, or collect apps. Never turn a destructive action into a joke.

## Experience principles

1. Start with a recognizable need and a concrete example.
2. Ask for a description in ordinary language.
3. Show meaningful progress and an honest way to stop.
4. Make the result easy to open and easy to revise.
5. Explain costs before paid requests and deletion before it happens.

These principles guide future product work. This branding package does not implement that work.

## Product reality

The inspected build is an early Android app with Claude API generation, local project files, an in-app browser, follow-up editing, and manual deletion. OpenAI support is disabled pending verification. Users supply their own provider key; usage is billed by that provider. The app's own pricing and distribution plans are not established here.

The consumer ambition and current setup have a real gap. Marketing for the current build must disclose the key and billing requirement before someone commits to trying it. Do not use “anyone can start immediately” as a substitute for improving onboarding.

## How to evaluate the brand

In a short moderated test, show the headline, an example, and the setup disclosure to people without development or AI experience. Ask them to explain what the product makes, where the result runs, whether it disappears, and what is required to start. Record their words rather than coaching them.

Treat comprehension, first useful result, and successful revision as the useful signals. Automatic tracking, retention targets, and analytics collection are not authorized or implemented by this document.
